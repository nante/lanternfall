## D2. Actors, art and magic

All art is authored by Opus. First ask Opus to make original, directly editable pixel PNG sheets, not borrow or trace reference art. Target a 32×48 hooded hero and 40×32 thornboar: four directions, one idle and two walk frames per direction, with side mirroring only if it reads correctly. Add one hit pose per actor for the shown direction. Treat these 26 source frames as an upper bound, not a production catalogue. Add one tree, a lamp and one distant Spire silhouette. These are a quality and throughput test, not a claim that Claude can reliably draw polished sprites.

Use alpha-cut, nearest-filtered mesh planes anchored at the feet in a 3D world. Sample a single local light value at the foot and tint the sheet; painted highlights carry form. No normal/depth atlas or automatic bake initially. Compare the planes to today's box actors in the same place, through walls, a roof doorway and the one lower camera. If the low-pitch hero fails, use an Opus-authored separate vista-only silhouette; do not add a second entire atlas. Live shaped rigs are a fallback experiment if Opus cannot produce readable sheet art; their ability to receive light and take arbitrary angles is real, but it does not guarantee an authored fable look. Full 8-direction bake, normals and `gl_FragDepth` are too much machinery before the images have won.

Magical means more than orange light. Give the Spire a tall, slightly impossible lantern shape and a slow breathing turquoise rune; a small turquoise wisp drifts toward it over the river. Both are original art, visibly rare against amber fires and blue-black night, and their motion is a few discrete pixel frames. They are decorative: they do not light tiles or expose hidden creatures. A later gameplay wisp must be an explicit server source. Distant scale, strange silhouette and warm inhabited windows express an inhabited fable without adding lore systems.

## D3. Movement and impact

Retain my round-one foot-anchor snap: the engine changes the tile before its next-step deadline (`packages/game/src/engine.ts:460`), while the current client starts a new interpolation when a snapshot arrives (`apps/client/src/render/view.ts:331`). Interpolating across the previous tile visually contradicts a strict server-tile reading. Snap position and picking to the received tile; cycle two walk poses, a one-pixel bob and dust inside it. On floor changes and missing or coalesced snapshots, snap. This may feel jerky; compare it with existing motion, but do not adopt smooth crossing without changing the stated contract. Impact, miss and spell flashes are shown only when an effect arrives; cosmetic hit-stop lasts 40–60 ms, offsets stay within 0.1 tile, and reduced motion turns these off. A held or absent pose is preferable to inventing a server-confirmed death or wind-up event.

## D4. Light rule and clock

Start with a free toggled test lantern, unchanged AI and no shades, fuel, regeneration penalty or shield-slot trade. Underground ambient is zero; surface day is lit and night is dim. Use four integer light bands and Chebyshev distance. A source contributes `max(0, strength - distance)`, bounded by its radius; strongest wins, with a stable source-ID tie break for color. Initial carried lantern strength 5/radius 4; a fixed lamp strength 4/radius 3. Only same-floor sources count, and a shared grid ray stops at sight-blocking tiles and closed doors. Brightness and flicker cannot change the authoritative visibility threshold. Doors are explicitly new: current sight does not include their dynamic state (`packages/game/src/world.ts:61`).

A creature is perceivable if it is in unobstructed sight and either its tile's band is positive or it is within one tile (touch). Touch does not see through a wall. Unseen creatures are absent from `Engine.view`, cannot be targeted and cease to drive automatic chase; ordinary walking, healing and blind area effects remain possible. An area effect can hit without revealing an unseen target's identity or location. Filter battle list, map markers, effects, hover and look results consistently. The current target and view paths accept and send nearby monsters without this rule (`engine.ts:277`, `:636`), so darkness requires a real server rule, not a post effect. In the limited prototype run one real local `Engine` with an opt-in rule, using two observer views in that engine. Before shipping, audit all actions, effects, players, corpses, reconnects, server persistence and races; do not treat the fixture as multiplayer security.

Later, separately test a five-minute lantern fuel unit and a cave beetle that acquires a lit player two tiles farther away. That creates a sight-versus-attention tradeoff; neither is in the first rule.

Use a 40-minute active-time cycle on existing `state.time`, proposed 18 day, 4 dusk, 14 night, 4 dawn; the server pauses time when empty (`apps/server/src/index.ts:62`). Lamps stay lit, consistent with existing world text; only their apparent pools change as ambient falls. At dusk windows warm and the land turns copper/teal; night shifts to indigo, with moon and stars in the scenic composition. The field and palettes derive from the same phase; windows, reflection strips and cloud shadows do not grant gameplay light.
