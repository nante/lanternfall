## D1 — Perspective and grandeur

**Verdict: adopt part.** Normal play stays at the current steep orthographic angle, with a small number of discrete zoom sizes. An explicit lookout action at the north bridge opens a non-interactive, real low-angle view of a small staged foreground, with an original painted sky and distant Lantern Spire; input or combat immediately restores the play camera. A 35° perspective camera around 15° above the ground is a starting experiment, not a promise that the present town will look good at that angle. Compare it in the same picker with a fixed, full-width painted lookout composition. The camera view is worth testing because it keeps a real bridge foreground and makes the user's idea tangible; the painting is cheaper, easier to compose, and controls what the user actually sees. I change my round-1 preference for only a painted panel by adopting the Opus reports' proposed real lookout camera **as a bounded comparison**.

I do not adopt a single zoom-driven transition from near-top-down combat to horizon everywhere. At the overlook show a single staged composition, with one original immense Spire, painted ridges and large moon rather than recreating the dragon or castle. ## Engine and D5 — pixel discipline

Keep Three.js 0.187 (`package.json:27`). Existing `WorldView` has the orthographic camera, low-resolution depth target and post pass (`apps/client/src/render/view.ts:61`, `:103`, `:115`), roof and door handling (`view.ts:504`), and ground picking (`view.ts:248`). A switch would rebuild `update(view)`, picking, `applyStyle`, floors, roof cutaways, labels, hover and effects with no demonstrated missing capability. Three's installed WebGPU lighting addons import `three/webgpu` (`node_modules/three/examples/jsm/lighting/ClusteredLighting.js:1`); existing terrain patches use GLSL `onBeforeCompile` (`apps/client/src/render/terrain.ts:95`). WebGPU is not a free switch. Use one fork `apps/client/proto/_next/`, leaving `apps/client/src/render/` alone.

Adopt Opus xhigh's diagnosis that mixed pixel grids deserve a test, but not the claim that density alone causes the current look. Ground snaps at 24 samples per tile (`terrain.ts:101`); the canvas is stretched to its host (`apps/client/src/style.css:31`) and zoom is continuous (`view.ts:151`). Start at roughly 24 internal pixels per tile and 300–360 rows at the default zoom; fit a nearest-neighbour canvas at an integer **physical** pixel scale and letterbox the remainder, accounting for device pixel ratio and pointer coordinates. Discrete play zoom presets avoid continuous resampling; a change of camera framing may change pixels per tile, so do not claim every authored texel remains one screen pixel at every zoom. Test real 1× and 2× device scales, resize, roof and picking. During the brief vista transition some shimmer is acceptable; the settled view must be crisp. Begin with a limited authored palette, less dithering, less grass and outline, not an OKLab palette LUT or a promise of a specific color count. Try a LUT only if the images call for it.

Replace the eight nearest unshadowed point lights (`view.ts:12`, `:129`) and the free cave torch (`view.ts:229`) in the fork with a nearest-sampled, server-provided tile light field. Material sampling of field color/brightness shades ground and props, and sprite materials sample at their foot. Daylight keeps one directional shadow if affordable; cave ambient is zero and actor emissive `actorPop` is removed (`apps/client/src/render/actors.ts:6`). The field gives coarse, wall-occluded tile shadows, not the height-correct prop shadows in the flashlight reference. Six shadow faces per point light make many shadowed lights costly; only benchmark a single carried shadow light later if the coarse field fails. No ray-marched fog or bloom; a few restrained painted mist pixels are optional decoration, never a rule source.

## D4 — Light rule, day and night

**v1:** use four deterministic bands, Chebyshev radius (`packages/game/src/model.ts:8`), strongest source wins, deterministic color tie break. Day outside is lit; the roofed interior has no outdoor ambient and the undercroft has zero ambient. At night the outdoor ambient is zero. Start with a 3-tile carried test lantern, 4-tile street lamp, 3-tile altar/brazier; their center is brightest and falloff reaches zero outside radius. All source light stays on its floor. Street lamps and braziers remain lit day and night; I adopt Terra B's choice because it preserves the existing description that the lamp never goes out (`engine.ts:546`) and avoids a separate lamp schedule in v1. Windows are decorative, not sources.

A viewer sees a dynamic entity only with clear line and a lit tile, or within one tile with clear line. Adjacent visibility represents touch, not magical light. A hidden creature has no eyes-only proxy, and its AI and regeneration are unchanged. The player's own tile and nearby walls remain readable for navigation but are not additional rule light. The free lantern toggle isolates whether lost warning makes darkness scary; no fuel, shield slot, eyes, regen penalty or AI changes in v1. Later test a 5-minute fuel unit and +1 tile beetle acquisition of a lit carrier separately, with a visible warning. This follows the useful light-as-risk idea in the Opus reports while declining to bundle several balance changes into the first experiment.

Put the clock, source/field calculation, door-aware visibility and opt-in toggle in `packages/game` and let one Engine own them; production defaults off. A closed door blocks light and view; an open door passes them. Use a specified supercover grid line: if a ray crosses an exact diagonal corner, either opaque side blocks it; opaque endpoints are lit/visible but block anything beyond. Do not silently change existing monster AI sight: `Geography.sight` does not currently check door state (`packages/game/src/world.ts:61`; `model.ts:13`). Another player's carried source contributes to the same field.

`Engine.view()` must omit hidden monsters, players, corpses and ground items, and deliver a bounded per-tile color/intensity field and viewer visibility mask; the client masks static NPCs and labels too. Reject hidden-ID targeting and targeted casts, clear lost targets and automatic chase while preserving manual paths; audit `look`, NPC interaction, ground/corpse access, battle list, minimap, notices and effects including `from` so no hidden position leaks. Area spells can damage an unseen creature, but do not emit its identifying hit position to that viewer; feedback on the player remains. Static terrain is already distributed, so this is a rule about dynamic information, not secrecy of the map. The current target handler has no visibility check (`engine.ts:277`), the current view is only distance filtered (`engine.ts:636`), and the HUD uses view monsters for the battle list (`apps/client/src/main.ts:246`). The full audit is required before this becomes real multiplayer behavior; brightness settings cannot expand what the server sends.

Derive a **40-minute active simulation cycle** from `state.time` (18 day, 4 dusk, 14 night, 4 dawn), adopting Terra B's smaller cycle in place of my 48-minute round-1 proposal. The server stops ticking when nobody is present or combat-locked (`apps/server/src/index.ts:62`), so the clock intentionally pauses. Quantize server ambient; smoothly interpolate only decorative color between transitions without changing the visibility mask. Day is straw and moss, dusk copper and violet, night blue-black with warm amber sources, dawn pink to gold. Show time at the steep camera through ground/roof colors, lamp pools and a few water glints; moon and stars appear in the lookout. A time scrubber advances the local Engine, not a second client clock.

## The fable, not just a lighting filter

One quiet, recurring turquoise wisp follows a slow loop beside the shrine. It is a decorative emissive silhouette, not a light source and never reveals hidden entities. The Great Lantern's original uneven, hand-authored silhouette and engraved crescent motif recur on one lamp and the bridge; one dim rune gives a hint of old magic. A confirmed spell releases three amber or turquoise pixels that fade inside already visible space; it does not change rule illumination in v1. Big areas of quiet color, a few deliberate large trees, lantern gold and blue night give room for these details. Wonder comes from a world that seems inhabited and ancient, not a map full of particle effects.

## Risks and measurements

**Performance, unverified:** run the same 60-second town, cave and bridge route on a named MacBook-class integrated GPU at 1080p and 1440p, noting browser, DPR, internal resolution, actor/source counts and warmup. Aim for frame-time p95 ≤16.7 ms and 1% low ≥45 fps; capture all passes, baseline and candidate. Avoid `gl.finish()` as the sole GPU measure; use supported asynchronous GPU timing or browser traces and report unsupported counters. Start with one 1024 shadow, no point shadows and the low-res field; reduce decorative grass, mist and shadow resolution before legibility. On the server, 16 clients is the room maximum (`apps/server/src/index.ts:38`); measure whole tick plus all view generation and actual serialized bandwidth, not merely a microbenchmark. Invalidate field caches on source movement, doors, phases and rollback; transactions replace/restore state (`index.ts:33`). These are targets, not measured claims.

**Readability/accessibility:** retain self, an adjacent threat, nearby wall edges and the selected tile in dark; test a dim display and bright room. Brightness and contrast may lift allowed pixels, never reveal server-hidden entities; shape and value distinguish light from hazards, and reduced motion/flash is available. Roof and billboard depth, time-phase pops, and the staged camera's restored picking require explicit checks.

**Art and compatibility:** four complete directions, frame count, scenic variants and revisions are substantial Opus work, not free because there is no human artist. Time the first 20 frames and reject or simplify if they fail at native scale. The current server parses stored state and can reset after a failed parse (`apps/server/src/index.ts:20`); production rollout would need backward-compatible defaults or a tested migration. No production activation is part of these prototypes.
