## D1 — Perspective: adopt part, at one overlook

In normal play use the existing steep 50° orthographic camera with a few discrete zoom stops. At the north bridge, the outward zoom stop can enter a **scenic view**; an explicit Look button offers the same action. Switch through a short fade to a low perspective camera, initially around 15° above the ground with a 35° field of view. Frame the actual player small against an Opus-painted sky, far hills and an enormous original Spire. Movement, attack, a visible nearby threat, damage or Exit immediately returns to play; picking and loot are disabled in the scenic view. Simulation continues. Reduced motion cuts directly between views. The zoom control must communicate that its last stop is a non-interactive lookout, not a new combat range.

This is a **partial adoption**, not the proposed continuous single-camera transition across the whole map. There is no promised near-top-down zoom-in mode yet, and the perspective projection changes at the lookout. Ref-5 shows a rider below eye level and a huge figure across a sky; I changed my round-one fixed-panel position to test an actual lower view, adopting the overlook idea from both Opus reports. The fixed painted lookout panel remains the cheaper comparison if this view cannot pass.

The present camera is orthographic (`apps/client/src/render/view.ts:64`), and its angle and position are rebuilt every frame (`view.ts:481`). Orthographic projection does not shrink distant objects with distance ([Three.js camera documentation](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/OrthographicCamera.html)); just tilting it does not reproduce ref-5's perspective. A low view also exposes map edges, undersides, billboard angles and missing background. The 84×68 map and north bridge are finite (`packages/game/src/town.ts:3`, `:41`, `:62`), so the prototype stages one camera direction, masks exposed edges with painted scenery and makes no claim that the whole world works near the horizon. A fade and a second camera are simpler and more honest than promising a seamless orthographic-to-perspective camera. Test that actor silhouettes and depth ordering survive this one transition before widening its scope.

## Engine and D5 — pixel discipline

Keep Three.js 0.187 (`package.json:27`) and its WebGL2 renderer. The current view already provides a low-resolution target, floor changes, roof hiding, labels, raycast picking and the `update`, `onPick` and `applyStyle` interface (`apps/client/src/render/view.ts:103`, `:115`, `:191`, `:250`, `:300`, `:504`). Replacing it with a 2D engine or Babylon would require recreating those contracts without a demonstrated capability gain. WebGPU is also unnecessary for this field; Three's current [migration documentation](https://threejs.org/manual/pages/webgpurenderer) says `ShaderMaterial` and `onBeforeCompile` customizations must be ported to node materials/TSL.

Copy the needed renderer modules once to `apps/client/proto/_next/`. Remove the candidate's eight pooled point lights and free cave torch (`view.ts:129`, `:229`, `:499`), and actor self-glow (`apps/client/src/render/actors.ts:6`); retain one measured directional sun shadow. Use a small, world-aligned nearest-sampled tile light texture to shade ground, walls and sprite feet. It is derived from the same authoritative field used for visibility in a real deployment; its shadows are tile-shaped, not height-correct prop shadows. No shadowed point lights, volumetric fog or bloom smear in the first build. In installed Three, a point-light shadow renders six cube faces (`node_modules/three/src/renderers/webgl/WebGLShadowMap.js:287`); compare one only later if the cheaper result is insufficient.

I adopt Opus xhigh's **mixed density as a useful hypothesis**, not a proven sole cause of the current look. The ground source has 24 texels per world tile (`apps/client/src/render/textures.ts:48`), while the live style uses a 7.2-unit view height and 480-row target setting (`content/render-style.json:3`). Start the candidate around **320 internal rows and 24 render pixels per world unit horizontally** at its reference zoom. This requires a roughly 13.3-unit vertical camera span, a much wider view than today's 7.2; check actor legibility and visible area instead of silently shrinking them. Perspective and the ground's pitch foreshorten axes, so these figures do not make every ground texel or sprite pixel one screen pixel.

Use nearest filtering and fit the actual drawing buffer to an integer physical-pixel multiplier where display size and DPR permit; center with bars and map pointer coordinates and labels to the actual canvas rectangle. Check browser zoom and 1×/2× DPR rather than claiming perfect scaling on every display. The current CSS stretches the canvas to its host (`apps/client/src/style.css:31`), so it does not guarantee that property. Play zoom uses discrete stops; the lookout has a stable pixelated endpoint. Use a small Opus-chosen palette and reduced noisy grass first; a palette LUT and exact 24-pixel lock across zooms are deferred until they prove necessary.

## D4 — light rule, darkness and time

**The proposed first server rule:** in `packages/game`, define one same-floor light field for the shared Engine. Use bands 0–3 and Chebyshev distance (`packages/game/src/model.ts:8`). The strongest source wins; use a stable source ID as the color tie break. A free test lantern has strength 5, fixed lamps/braziers strength 4, with contribution `min(3, max(0, strength − distance))` after occlusion: their positive radii are respectively four and three tiles. Every player's switched-on lantern contributes. Surface ambient is 3 by day, 2 at dusk/dawn and **0 at night**; underground ambient is always 0. Night terrain can still be painted faint blue for orientation: this visual floor is not rule illumination and does not reveal actors.

Light and viewer sight use the same new, deterministic grid-ray helper. Closed doors and opaque terrain block; open doors do not. Show a blocking wall or closed door at the endpoint but never propagate beyond it. For a ray crossing an exact diagonal corner, block it if either adjoining orthogonal tile is opaque, including touch range. This is deliberately stricter than existing sight, which skips both endpoints, checks rounded intermediate tiles, and ignores door state (`packages/game/src/world.ts:61`; `packages/game/src/model.ts:13`). Test the distinction; do not silently claim existing combat or monster sight already behaves this way.

A creature is perceivable if it has a clear viewer-to-creature ray and either occupies a positively lit tile or is within Chebyshev distance 1. Touch does not see through walls. A hidden creature is not sent or targetable; losing visibility clears its target and automatic chase route while manual walking continues (`engine.ts:557`). Healing and blind area spells remain possible; filter hidden-target identifying effects and origins. Keep AI unchanged. Darkness therefore removes advance warning, not the player's controls. No eyes/shades, shield penalty, fuel, regeneration penalty or new aggro in v1.

For actual multiplayer integration, put source state, time, visibility and target checks in the authoritative Engine, not a client-only shader. Audit `view`, target acceptance, targeted spells, chase every tick, look, effects and their origins, battle list, minimap, other players, NPCs, corpses, items, reconnect and save compatibility. Current `view()` sends nearby entities/effects (`engine.ts:636`), target accepts a live same-floor monster (`engine.ts:277`), and `describe` uses screen proximity (`engine.ts:520`). Static NPCs come from the map (`view.ts:346`), so simply filtering `monsters` is insufficient. Preserve self damage feedback and avoid using a visibility filter as a claim of full secrecy for already distributed map data. The prototype below deliberately exercises only a restricted subset; it is not the server implementation.

Later, separately test a five-minute fuel unit and a beetle whose normal acquisition radius increases by two tiles for a lit player. Light would buy warning at the cost of fuel and attention. Current acquisition is based on range and sight (`engine.ts:581`); neither the tradeoff nor its balance is proven.

Use a proposed **40-minute active simulation cycle**: 18 day, 4 dusk, 14 night, 4 dawn. Derive phase from `state.time`; it pauses when the existing server pauses for lack of connected or combat-locked players (`apps/server/src/index.ts:62`). Lamps remain lit, matching the existing description (`engine.ts:546`); windows are decorative. Under the steep camera, phase reads through ground/roof color, gold lamp pools and water glints; moon and stars appear in the scenic view. The visual grade may ease, but visibility changes at the server band boundary. Clock controls in the prototype operate on its one local Engine, not on a separate simulated clock.

## Performance, readability and rollout risk

No timing, art-throughput or GPU claim here is measured. On a named MacBook-class integrated GPU, compare the same 60-second town/cave route in baseline and candidate at 1080p and 1440p, recording browser, DPR, buffer size, actors, shadow settings and all render passes. Aim for 60 fps median and ≥45 fps 1% low; report GPU timings only if supported. Lower grass, particles and shadow resolution first if needed. A low-resolution scene target does not make shadow maps, full-screen output, geometry or CPU free.

For eventual server integration, measure field calculation, serialization and view generation at the existing room limit of 16 clients and against the 50 ms tick (`apps/server/src/index.ts:38`, `:62`). Cache only with correct invalidation for doors, sources, movement, phase and transaction rollback; no source-count cap may silently alter gameplay. The actual field representation and payload are unmeasured. Offer brightness/contrast for already permitted terrain and actors, a stable self/adjacent-threat/selection outline, and reduced motion/flash.
