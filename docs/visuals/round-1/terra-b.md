Keep Three.js and make a small, authored 2.5D fable: crisp painted characters and scenery, warm lantern pools against a legible blue-black night, and one dramatic river overlook. Prove the art and tile interaction first, then test a server-owned light-and-visibility rule in one local Engine. Favor an inexpensive tile light field over a new engine or many shadow-casting lights; preserve the existing multiplayer rules and migrate nothing into production until a prototype wins.

## 1. Engine: extend Three.js

Keep the installed Three.js 0.187.0 (`package.json:27`). Its WebGL renderer requires WebGL2 (`node_modules/three/src/renderers/WebGLRenderer.js:62`). A switch would need to rebuild the current orthographic camera, low-resolution target, depth and picking, and implement `update(view)`, `onPick`, `applyStyle`, floor changes, roof hiding and DOM labels before it proves a better picture. Those responsibilities already exist in `apps/client/src/render/view.ts:61`, `:191`, `:250`, `:300`, `:460` and `:504`. No competing engine offers a needed capability that justifies that cost yet.

Fork the relevant renderer into a prototype-local module; later port only the winning changes. Replace busy ground detail, noisy grass, box actors and unconditional actor glow. Keep the low-resolution target, nearest sampling, depth-tested geometry, the directional sun and a restrained ambient fill (`view.ts:104`, `:115`, `:123`). Use a server-produced tile light field as the authoritative source for colored local illumination: sample it by world tile in the terrain material and at the actor's foot tile for cards. This replaces, rather than adds to, the eight unshadowed local point lights for the darkness experiment; emissive flame pixels are decorative and cannot reveal a creature. Keep sunlight shadows where useful and affordable. Remove the automatic cave torch (`view.ts:499`) and tune down the cave's fixed ambient (`:211`).

The field stops at opaque tiles and closed doors, producing hard, pixel-stepped occlusion. It does not produce height-correct shadows of every fence or prop; that is an explicit compromise against the flashlight reference. Three.js can cast point-light shadows ([PointLight documentation](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/PointLight.html)), but this installed version renders six cube faces per shadowed point light (`node_modules/three/src/renderers/webgl/WebGLShadowMap.js:246`, `:287`). Do not make eight lights cast them. If the tile mask feels flat, separately benchmark one optional low-resolution shadowed torch as decoration, never as the gameplay visibility oracle. No volumetric ray march or blurred bloom; use a few restrained pixel-stepped haze bands. Reserve sky for the overlook.

## 2. Actors and art

Use hand-pixelled sheets on upright, camera-facing **mesh planes** anchored at the authoritative foot tile. Keep walls, doors and roofs as 3D geometry; use alpha-cut painted planes for a few trees and props. Plan eight facing directions, mirroring where asymmetric gear permits. A complete character budget is 1 idle + 4 walk + 3 attack + 1 hurt + 3 death frames per direction: up to 96 source frames before mirroring and equipment variants. This is an art budget, not a promise to produce them in the prototype.

Start with one original hooded traveler and one bristled thornboar: four cardinal directions, idle, two walk frames and one hit pose; mirror sides where appropriate. Draw at 32 × 48 pixels for the human and approximately 40 × 32 for the boar, using 4–6 values per material and a warm/cool limited palette. Use simple manually edited pixel grids, not generated lookalike assets. Judge silhouettes in the actual game viewport. Hold missing poses rather than inventing attacks or making a whole catalogue.

The authored colors provide form; the light-field sample uniformly tints/dims each card and the ground gets per-tile lighting. Do not author normal maps yet: the camera-facing normal would make their lighting unintuitive and the art burden is substantial. A custom or modified mesh material samples the field; ordinary `SpriteMaterial` does not supply this custom illumination. Use nearest filtering and alpha test with depth writing for cutouts, and test front/behind wall and roof ordering, labels and click targets. Three.js's installed Lambert material supports alpha-tested maps and normal maps if later needed (`node_modules/three/src/materials/MeshLambertMaterial.js:59`, `:169`), but neither automatically creates the proposed tile field.

## 3. Light as a rule

**First rule:** a monster on the same floor can be seen and selected if it is adjacent to the player, or if its tile is illuminated and the player has line of sight. Adjacency uses the game's existing Chebyshev distance and existing sight semantics, including their current diagonal behavior (`packages/game/src/model.ts:7`, `packages/game/src/world.ts:61`); test corners explicitly. Outside that safety radius, no light means fewer seconds of warning as a creature approaches. Walking, healing and the map remain usable. A hidden enemy cannot be selected or followed; losing sight clears its target and its automatic chase route, while a manual walk continues. The current `dropTarget` already distinguishes the two (`packages/game/src/engine.ts:557`).

Proposed starting values, to be tuned: radius 4 tiles for an amber carried lantern, radius 3 for a street lamp or brazier, and day ambient sufficient to reveal outdoors; underground ambient is zero for this rule. Use a deterministic, discrete falloff with a positive illumination threshold. For the first rule, take the strongest qualifying source per tile; sources do not stack. A source affects its own floor only, and a grid ray stops at existing opaque terrain or a closed door. The door part is **new**: existing `Geography.sight` neither receives door state nor blocks doors (`packages/game/src/model.ts:14`, `packages/game/src/world.ts:61`). Implement and test an explicit light-occlusion helper; don't claim current sight does it. The same field includes another player's lantern. A soft visual fringe can exist inside the lit boundary but never imply that a server-hidden enemy is selectable.

The gameplay implementation belongs in `packages/game` and the server's single authoritative state: source definitions, fuel, shared phase, per-tile illumination, visibility predicate and a compact viewport-sized light field/result in `GameView`. Validate target commands and targeted spells, clear stale targets before any dependent chase or attack, and filter monster snapshots and monster-identifying look results consistently. The current target action accepts any living monster on the floor (`engine.ts:277`); `view` sends all nearby living monsters (`:636`); `describe` checks screen proximity (`:520`). A shaded picture alone does none of this. Remove hidden monsters from battle list, hover, picking and minimap; filter or redact effects and origins that would disclose their positions. Keep map terrain knowledge separate: this proposal hides monsters, not explored map geometry. Blind area spells may still hit an unseen creature; suppress its identifying hit effect until visible, but show the caster's own burst. Recheck all command and event paths at implementation, including races when a source goes out.

**Cost and behavior, after the first visibility test:** give the lantern five minutes per fuel unit, show remaining fuel and a low-fuel warning, and make it extinguish authoritatively. A lit lantern attracts cave beetles: add two tiles to their existing acquisition range when the player carries a lit source, subject to existing same-floor/line-of-sight checks; other creatures remain unchanged. Thus light buys warning at the cost of fuel and greater beetle attention. This is an intentional second rule, not a claim that the current AI already behaves this way (`engine.ts:581`). There is currently no lantern, fuel item or equipment slot in the IDs (`packages/game/src/ids.ts:8`); adding and persisting them, migration of existing saves, and trading/refueling are real follow-on work. Do not silently reset old saves: server restore currently falls back if schema parsing fails (`apps/server/src/index.ts:20`). The first prototype uses a free on/off lantern to isolate visibility; it does not pretend fuel or beetle AI are implemented.

## 4. Day and night

Use a 40-minute **active simulation** cycle: 18 minutes day, 4 dusk, 14 night, 4 dawn. Derive the shared phase from server simulation time, sent in the view; it pauses when no player is present, matching the current server tick policy (`apps/server/src/index.ts:62`), rather than pretending to track real-world time. Prototype controls can advance that one Engine's time for demonstration. Persist phase via the existing time field and verify restart behavior before shipping.

Day is moss, straw and cool shadows; dusk is copper against teal; night is dark indigo with amber lamps; dawn begins violet and warms to gold. Existing street lamps are described as never going out (`packages/game/src/engine.ts:546`), so leave them lit: their pools become noticeable as ambient fades. Windows may brighten at dusk as decorative art; only explicitly identified lamps and braziers are gameplay sources. Underground stays dark regardless of phase. From the steep camera, time reads through ground color, shadow direction, lit windows, reflection strips and a slow passing cloud shadow. Show a moon and stars in the overlook's sky, not in normal top-down play.

## 5. Grandeur without a new world

Stage one overlook around the existing north river bridge (`packages/game/src/town.ts:41`, `:62`). Frame the tiny traveler with dark trees; put the bridge and water in the middle; paint an original distant mountain, towers and a bright sky behind. Separate foreground, middle and distance with two coarse haze bands. One shrine tower 4–6 human heights tall makes scale legible without changing collision tiles. Distant silhouettes are scenery, not promised reachable locations.

Use a short, skippable, non-interactive scenic composition on reaching a safe overlook, with a lower framing and a painted sky panel; simulation continues and any movement or combat returns immediately to normal view. Do not expose extra interactable tiles or put the panel over essential combat information. The normal playable view keeps its existing camera and picking. This is a targeted proof of wonder, not a general dynamic horizon, an enormous map, or an over-the-shoulder combat mode.

## 6. Feel and weight under server timing

The engine assigns the destination tile immediately and sets the next step time (`packages/game/src/engine.ts:460`); the current client then interpolates from the old position after receiving the snapshot (`apps/client/src/render/view.ts:331`, `:442`). That interpolation visibly trails the authoritative tile. For this experiment, snap the actor's foot anchor and selection marker to the received tile; animate squash, cloak and feet within that tile, with at most a small sub-tile pose offset. Use ground- and diagonal-dependent timing from `packages/game/src/rules.ts:35`, and snap on correction or floor change. This deliberately prioritizes truthful location over smooth sliding; test whether the gait still feels good.

On a received hit or miss, use a 40–60 ms **cosmetic** hold on the affected sprite, a one-pixel recoil and sparse debris; never freeze simulation, health, selection or other actors. A confirmed spell gives a brief quantized flash; damage is already applied when the current ember effect is emitted (`engine.ts:361`), so present impact immediately and any projectile as an after-image, not as a later supposed hit. Current effects contain no attack-start or death event (`packages/game/src/model.ts:70`): don't invent pre-impact wind-up. A corpse/removed monster snapshot can trigger a short death pose only after confirmation; if that proves ambiguous, add a server event in a later integration. Reduced motion disables shake and flashes while retaining readable hit/miss indicators. Sound can wait for the visual decision.

## 8. Risks and budgets

Treat performance as unverified. Target a MacBook-class integrated GPU at 1920 × 1080 and 2560 × 1440 physical output: at least 60 fps median and 45 fps 1st percentile over a 60-second route containing the town, undercroft, multiple actors and combat. Record Mac model, browser, display scale, viewport, actor count, internal target dimensions and whether sun shadows are on. Report frame-time distribution and main-thread cost; use GPU timing only where supported. Measure baseline and prototype with the same scene. A 30 fps median is a fallback investigation threshold, not a success claim. Server-side, measure 16 local simulated clients and source-heavy scenes against its 50 ms tick budget (`apps/server/src/index.ts:62`); field size, serialization cost and bandwidth are not yet measured.

Aim for roughly 320–360 internal pixels high, with an integer physical-pixel upscale and letterboxing when necessary; test 1× and 2× device scale, resizing and pointer mapping. The existing code rounds a CSS-height ratio and stretches the canvas (`view.ts:236`, `apps/client/src/style.css:31`), so it does **not** guarantee uniform physical pixel widths. Reducing render resolution alone does not eliminate geometry, material, shadow or CPU cost. If budget fails, reduce particles, shadow resolution and decorative detail before reducing readable actor size.

Accessibility is not the same as expanding vision. Provide a brightness/contrast adjustment for already permitted pixels, plus stable outlines for self, adjacent enemies, tile selection and exit; it must not cause hidden actors to be sent or selected. Offer reduced flash/motion and symbols as well as color for fuel. In a dark-display test, participants must identify self, a neighboring threat, the ladder and the selected tile, then escape using keyboard and mouse. Art is the largest schedule unknown: a few hand-crafted sprites may take substantially longer than the code. Reject or simplify the direction if a non-artist cannot produce a readable hero and boar within the first slice.

## 7. Ordered prototypes

Estimates below are rough developer-days, including rudimentary original art and manual validation; none is measured. Each lives entirely under `apps/client/proto/`, imports the real shared Engine and has no production imports pointing back into it, like `apps/client/proto/chrome/main.ts:6`.

1. **Fable slice — engine decision** (4–6 days). Add `apps/client/proto/fable/{index.html,main.ts,style.css,view.ts}` and local actor/terrain modules and pixel sheets. Run one real `Engine`; render the shrine and a boar at dusk with the new painted hero. Proves art direction and Three.js depth/picking. Pass when a viewer identifies hero and boar at actual size, the selected tile matches movement, a roof hides on entry and returns on exit, and a floor transition places the player correctly. Compare with `/proto/chrome/` at the same viewport. If the scene cannot achieve the intended fable with these assets and budget, reconsider the renderer before building more.
2. **Lantern and shadow** (4–7 days). Add `apps/client/proto/lantern/{index.html,main.ts,light-engine.ts,view.ts}` and reuse the prototype art. Implement a prototype-local `Engine` subclass: one instance owns a free switchable lantern, computes illumination, guards target commands and returns filtered views. Keep this fixture deliberately limited to walking, selection and a scripted nearby monster; disable auto-chase and offensive spells in this demo rather than asserting that overriding only `view` makes inherited combat correct. Show a wall, a door, two player lanterns and the underground ladder. Pass when toggling light hides/reveals the enemy for each observer, direct target commands for a hidden enemy fail, adjacency works, a closed door and wall block light, and a second player's lantern reveals it. Test floor boundaries and diagonal corners. Before any multiplayer integration, implement and verify the full authoritative command, chase, spell, event and persistence paths above; the fixture is evidence, not production security.
3. **Cycle and overlook** (3–5 days). Add `apps/client/proto/overlook/{index.html,main.ts,view.ts}` plus local backdrop art. Use the local Engine's phase with accelerated preview controls; show day, dusk, night and dawn at the bridge, then the skippable vista. Pass when viewers correctly sequence the phases without text, see the moon in the night vista, identify the route at each phase and return to normal camera immediately on input. Check lamps remain actual sources and no panel obscures combat.
4. **Confirmed impact** (2–4 days). Add `apps/client/proto/impact/{index.html,main.ts,view.ts}` using the local Engine and existing effect data. Compare motion on/off. Pass when walk, confirmed hit, miss, spell and confirmed death read distinctly; an impact never precedes its event; and movement, hit feedback and floor changes keep the selection on the server-held tile under delayed and rapid snapshots.

Do not build the production migration, a complete sprite catalogue, normal maps, new world regions, dynamic point-shadow arrays, volumetric fog, fuel economy or monster-light AI during these visual prototypes.

## What changed in pass 2

1. **Engine:** Kept Three.js. Replaced the pass-1 combination of a mask plus eight point lights with one sampled tile field, avoiding contradictory double lighting. Verified point-light shadow cost in the installed renderer; documented the loss of height-correct shadows.
2. **Actors:** Kept authored cards, but specified mesh planes, the first four-direction/two-frame slice, depth ordering and real frame-count cost. Verified the material capabilities instead of assuming a normal map would work automatically.
3. **Light:** Narrowed the first experiment to free lantern visibility; moved fuel and one concrete beetle response to later. Added target, spell, chase, look, effect, multiplayer source and save-migration consequences; defined closed-door behavior because current sight ignores doors. The prototype explicitly disables inherited paths it cannot yet enforce.
4. **Day/night:** Kept the 40-minute cycle but made it active simulation time, explicitly paused when the existing server pauses. Kept lamps burning in keeping with current world text instead of turning them off in daylight.
5. **Grandeur:** Kept the river overlook but made it a skippable safe scenic composition with simulation continuing; this avoids implying the existing downward camera can naturally see a sky or exposing new gameplay space.
6. **Feel:** Replaced interpolated between-tile movement with authoritative foot anchoring, since the server commits the destination before the current interpolation starts. Clarified that the current spell effect arrives after damage and that death/wind-up events do not exist.
7. **Prototypes:** Kept four in the same order, but constrained the light fixture to behaviors one Engine subclass can actually guard, specified files and acceptance checks, and separated future production enforcement from visual proof.
8. **Risks:** Kept the frame-rate goals as unverified targets, added output/DPR and 16-client server measurements, and corrected the assumption that the current CSS upscale guarantees integer physical pixels. Clarified that accessible brightness never expands information or targeting.
