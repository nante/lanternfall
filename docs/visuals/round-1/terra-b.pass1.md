# Lanternfall visuals — independent proposal, pass 1

Keep Three.js and build a small authored 2.5D fable on top of the existing tile world: pixel-painted characters and landmarks, warm pools of light against a legible blue-black night, and a single dramatic overlook. Make darkness meaningful through one server-owned visibility rule before considering more complex monster behavior. Compare it in playable prototypes driven by one real local Engine; do not migrate production until the direction wins.

## 1. Engine

Keep Three.js 0.187.0 (`package.json:27`): replacing it would reimplement camera, low-resolution targets, depth, picking and the adapter to the game's views before proving the visual problem. `WorldView` already owns an orthographic camera, low-resolution target, nearest sampling and pixel-snapped camera (`apps/client/src/render/view.ts:61`, `:115`, `:236`, `:481`). It handles world picks, labels and floors (`:250`, `:300`, `:460`), and building roofs react to the authoritative player tile (`:504`). Preserve `update(view)`, `onPick`, `applyStyle`, floor transitions and labels in the eventual production integration.

The first prototype copies the relevant renderer into its own directory and replaces the noisy ground and box actors; it must not change the production renderer. Use Lambert-lit terrain, a directional sun, subdued hemisphere fill, and at most eight nearest unshadowed decorative point lights. Existing lights are pooled (`view.ts:12`, `:424`); current cave lighting is always on (`:211`, `:499`). Remove the fake all-screen shafts, excessive grass noise, automatic cave torch and unconditional actor emissive. For the test, calculate a low-resolution light mask from discrete sources and wall occlusion; apply it consistently to lit terrain and actor cards. Use tile-based occlusion, not multiple dynamic point-light shadow maps. Test a short 3D shadowed torch separately only if the mask fails to convey an obstruction. Keep gradients and fog quantized at internal resolution; use no blurred bloom or volumetric ray march. A short, authored sky/backdrop is reserved for the overlook, not the normal gameplay camera.

## 2. Actors and art pipeline

Replace characters and creatures with hand-authored pixel sheets on upright camera-facing planes, anchored to the tile at their feet. Keep solid geometry for walls and roof occlusion; draw props such as trees as pixel-painted cutout planes with a small solid trunk where appropriate. The fixed camera and 8-way movement justify eight directions: front, back, both sides and diagonals. For each direction aim for idle 1, walk 4, attack 3, hurt 1 and death 3 frames, with common reusable silhouettes and mirrored directions where silhouette and equipment permit. First prototype needs only a hero and one boar, four walk directions plus mirrored diagonals where credible, idle and hit; show missing frames as held poses, never fake a whole production asset library.

Target a 32 × 48 pixel human frame, feet anchored at a consistent pixel; boar about 40 × 32. Use 4–6 value bands per material with a small warm/cool palette. Without an artist, draw deliberately simple originals in a pixel editor or with an authored pixel grid: hood, cloak, boots, shield, a bristled boar. Test silhouettes at actual in-game size, with no textures generated from the references. For first iteration use flat shade plus the same sampled light mask as ground, with a narrow warm rim drawn into frames. Do not create normal maps until the simpler art works; cards should not become luminous in darkness. Use nearest filtering, alpha cutouts, depth test, predictable foot anchoring and a fixed consistent pixel grid. Painted scenery must not obscure a clickable tile or hide a target unfairly.

## 3. Light as a mechanic

Start with server-calculated world illumination by time, warm fixed lamps with radius, a carried lantern and minimal ambient light underground. Introduce a small authoritative visibility rule first: an enemy beyond one tile is visible and targetable only when its tile is illuminated above a shared threshold and line of sight exists. Adjacent enemies are always discernible and targetable, preserving a last-resort playable radius. Player movement and self-healing remain possible in the dark; ranged attacks cannot target a hidden enemy. Losing visibility clears the existing target and stops pursuit of that target; monsters do not get new behavior in this first change. A held lantern lights a radius of four tiles and consumes fuel, e.g. one unit per five minutes, with a clear low-fuel icon and extinguishing event. A nearby world lamp lights radius three. Light from another player should count as a shared source. Distinguish these initial numbers as prototype tuning, not proven balance.

Put the clock, fuel, sources, obstruction, target validation, target loss and per-player filtered views in `packages/game` and authoritative server execution, never in a competing client simulation. Current `Geography.sight` is a line-of-sight check, not an illumination rule (`packages/game/src/world.ts:61`), and the current snapshot supplies nearby monsters (`packages/game/src/engine.ts:636`). Client: render the server's light data and permitted entities; show local shape, fuel, light extent and clear target-lost feedback. Use the same source positions and occluder rules to avoid a shadow saying “hidden” while the server considers a tile lit. The client can interpolate intensity and flicker only within the server's lit boundary. Make targeting, hover, battle list and minimap follow the same visibility result. Darkness is frightening because unknown creatures may approach the lantern edge, not because the interface stops working.

A later controlled test could make one cave creature prefer dark routes, or flee a bright torch, but do not introduce several new AI systems or stat penalties at once. The player's decision is whether to spend scarce light now or risk shorter warning distance, not whether a darkness debuff silently changes damage.

## 4. Day and night

Use a 40-minute simulation cycle: 18 minutes day, 4 dusk, 14 night and 4 dawn. The server's world phase is shared by everyone; underground stays dark throughout. Day: moss and straw, pale cool shadows; dusk: copper highlights and turquoise shadows; night: deep blue with amber lanterns; dawn: misty violet warming to gold. Street lamps and windows visibly kindle across the dusk transition and dim at dawn. Window light is emissive art, street lamps are actual light sources; indicate those two roles clearly. Cycle time and phase should be inspectable in the prototype and accelerated there, but not independently guessed for rules.

Since the camera looks down, show time in the color and angle of shadows, warm windows, lantern pools, moonlit water and slow ground cloud shadows. Show moon and stars in the authored overlook backdrop only; don't add a permanent miniature sky behind a camera that points at the ground. Keep slow flame and cloud motion subtle and pixel-stepped.

## 5. Grandeur

Use one named overlook at the north river bridge: foreground framing trees and the player, middle-distance town roofs and a large shrine, and a distant painted original mountain/castle silhouette against a broad sky. Layer the artwork behind the playable terrain and use haze bands for separation. In that optional viewpoint, briefly lower the camera and widen the view on arrival; restore normal pitch on movement or input, never change pathing or expose hidden tiles. A slow cloud shadow and sparse fireflies suggest scale at normal camera angle. One unusually tall, 4–6-player-height shrine tower and a future larger-than-player creature sell relative scale more efficiently than a sprawling new map. Existing river and bridge give a concrete place to stage the shot (`packages/game/src/town.ts:41`, `:62`). Do not create a fake world-horizon panel that moves independently of the actual scene during ordinary navigation.

## 6. Feel and weight

The server writes the tile before the renderer interpolates to it, and duration depends on ground and diagonal travel (`packages/game/src/engine.ts:460`, `packages/game/src/rules.ts:35`, `apps/client/src/render/view.ts:331`). Keep hit tests and markers on the authoritative target tile; restrict visible motion to the current step interval and snap on floor change/correction. Within the tile, add one planted contact frame, slight body compression and a one-pixel dust puff at step end. Never invent an early impact.

React to received hit/miss/spell/death events with a 40–60 ms cosmetic pose hold on the affected sprite only, one-pixel recoil, small directional particles and a short, quantized flash. Do not freeze or delay simulation, other actors or authoritative health. Only play a wind-up when the server has supplied a real start/cast event; otherwise show an immediate post-confirmation strike pose. Current `Effect` contains an id, kind, tile, time and optional origin (`packages/game/src/model.ts:70`), and current effects arrive in snapshots (`view.ts:367`); test multiple events between snapshots and rapid corrections. Sound is not required to decide the visual direction; reserve a small sound hook for later. Offer reduced-motion mode disabling shake and flashes without hiding combat information.

## 8. Risks and acceptance budgets

Set a target, not a claim: MacBook integrated GPU, 1080p and 2560 × 1440 windows, 60 fps median and at least 45 fps 1st percentile during movement and a crowded battle; record device, browser, viewport, internal render size, scene population and 60-second samples. Also measure 30 fps minimum as a fallback under the same scene; investigate jank rather than promising the target before measurement. Record CPU frame time and GPU time where supported, with browser tooling, separately from simulation. Cap internal render height at roughly 360 pixels and no more than 8 nearby decorative lights, no shadow-casting point lights by default. At high DPI, verify the CSS and backing resolution explicitly; nearest scaling alone does not guarantee even pixel sizes.

With the lantern off, a player must identify their own tile, immediately adjacent enemy and an exit, and complete movement with keyboard as well as mouse. Give a brightness/accessibility option that lifts visible dark colors without expanding the server's visibility, plus a reduced-flash option; labels and combat indicators need contrast independent of color. Validate this with several users and a dim display, rather than assuming a numeric brightness works. Art cost is the main risk: 8 directions × 12 frames is 96 frames per actor before equipment; mirror and share poses, then only commit after the hero and boar pass a silhouette test.

## 7. Ordered prototypes

Do not build a full sprite catalogue, normal-map pipeline, renderer rewrite, world expansion, real volumetrics, lighting-dependent monster AI or a production migration yet. These estimates are rough engineering/art prototypes, not measured commitments.

1. **Fable slice / engine decision** — Add `apps/client/proto/fable/{index.html,main.ts,style.css}` and prototype-local renderer/art modules and sheets. Run a real local `Engine`, keep normal tile picking, roof hiding and floor change; display the shrine, hero and boar at dusk. Compare with `/proto/chrome/` at the same viewport. Pass if the original hero and boar are distinguishable at 1×, the roof opens at the authoritative tile, and mouse/keyboard steps land on the expected tile; the user must prefer its scene without relying on post-filter intensity. Choose Three.js if this meets the visual goal. Estimate 4–6 developer days; art time uncertain.
2. **Darkness / light** — Add `apps/client/proto/lantern/` with a prototype-local Engine subclass and rule adapter, not a second simulation or production import. Show fixed lamps, carried light with fuel, underground walls and one approaching creature. One local Engine instance owns the rule and emits the view; its visible result drives picking and the interface. Pass if an enemy outside the lit region disappears and cannot be targeted, adjacency still works, a wall blocks illumination, another carried lantern reveals it, and extinguishing clears target. Estimate 4–7 days including the coherent rule, UI and checks; production server migration is separate.
3. **Day/night plus overlook** — Add `apps/client/proto/overlook/`, reusing prototype-local fable assets, with accelerated phase controls and one original sky/city backdrop at the river bridge. Pass if observers correctly order day, dusk, night and dawn without a text label, and can still identify the player and route at every phase. Return to gameplay camera on input. Estimate 3–5 days, with illustration a material unknown.
4. **Combat weight** — Add `apps/client/proto/impact/` using the same local Engine and sprite sheet, plus prototype-local effect timing. Compare with motion off. Pass if a confirmed hit, miss, death and step are visually distinct, no impact precedes its effect event, and repeated steps, latency and floor transitions never leave the pick marker behind the authoritative tile. Estimate 2–4 days.
