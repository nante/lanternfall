Keep Three.js and build an original painted-pixel fable around three tests: a readable sprite hero and creature with tile-honest motion; server-owned pools of light that hide distant threats; and a river overlook with a vast painted landmark. Use warm lanterns against deep blue night, quiet ground textures and large silhouettes instead of stronger filters. Start with one small visibility rule, a free test lantern and unchanged creature AI; only add fuel and light-sensitive creatures after that rule is fun and legible. The prototypes use the real local Engine and stay outside the production renderer until a direction wins.

# Lanternfall visuals — Terra, round 1

## 1. Engine: keep Three.js

Keep the installed Three.js 0.187.0 (`package.json:27`). The game already needs real geometry for its floors, roof cutaways, depth and picking, and can render pixel sprites in that same scene. No required capability justifies a new engine. A switch would mean replacing `update(GameView)`, `onPick`, `applyStyle`, floor/roof handling and labels, as well as the less obvious `beforePick`, hover, `onEffect`, `tileAtPoint`, canvas and camera access used by the client (`apps/client/src/render/view.ts:78`, `:99`, `:248`, `apps/client/src/main.ts:267`). Revisit only if prototype 1 fails its visual, picking or performance gate after a targeted fix; it is the engine decision.

The current view already uses a low-resolution color/depth target and orthographic camera (`apps/client/src/render/view.ts:61`, `:103`), nearest filtering and a pixelated canvas (`view.ts:115`, `apps/client/src/style.css:31`). Preserve those, pixel-snapped camera movement and the directional shadow, but lower grass density, dithering, outlines, orange rim and global color grading (`view.ts:22`). Target 300–360 internal pixels high, integer nearest upscale where possible, letterboxing or consistent pixel-size rounding where it is not. The live style currently requests 480 (`content/render-style.json:5`), so do not assume the present resolution is already the budget.

For local light, replace the eight unconstrained Three point lights with a **tile light field**: one authoritative, occlusion-aware brightness/color value per tile, sampled by terrain and sprite materials. Use four intensity bands, a few source colors and small visual-only flame flicker. It deliberately casts tile-shaped light shadows, not physically accurate wall/prop shadows; prototype the visual result before adding complexity. Keep the directional sun and its shadow for the surface. There is no point-light shadow map in the first build. Three supports them, but its own renderer draws six cube faces per shadowing point light (`node_modules/three/src/renderers/webgl/WebGLShadowMap.js:287`; [Three.js shadow documentation](https://threejs.org/manual/pages/shadows.html)). If the tile shadows fail artistically, measure a single shadowed carried light as a later comparison, never eight by default. Use one or two slow, pixelated mist cards and a few dust pixels, not ray-marched volumetrics; sky is a painted vista at one lookout.

## 2. Actors and art pipeline

Use hand-pixelled sprite sheets on foot-anchored camera-facing planes, combined with the existing 3D terrain. Design for four cardinal facings. A diagonal step uses the nearer cardinal; on an exact tie keep the prior facing if possible. The actual tile and direction remain authoritative. Target a 32×48 hero and 48×32 boar, each with four facings and, per facing, one idle, four walk, two attack, one hit and one death frame: 36 frames per actor before mirror reuse. These are target dimensions, not proof of final on-screen scale.

Do **not** draw all 72 frames to decide the style. First make two facings each of the hero and thornboar: idle, two walk, one attack and one hit pose (20 small frames total), plus a simple corpse silhouette. A developer can hand-pixel those using 16–24 colors per character: a dark cobalt cloak, pale face/weapon and distinct feet for the hero; a low brown body, bristles and pale tusks for the boar. Replace one noisy tree and one lamp with deliberate, readable shapes. Do not copy reference assets. Time this work and view at the actual internal resolution before committing to the full sheet.

Use a nearest-filtered atlas and alpha-cutout, depth-tested planes. An actor samples the server's light field at its foot, tinted by source color; a small contact shadow grounds it. No normal maps initially: directional painted highlights and silhouette carry form. A stock `SpriteMaterial` is not automatically lit—the installed sprite shader outputs the texture color directly (`node_modules/three/src/renderers/shaders/ShaderLib/sprite.glsl.js:58`)—so the candidate needs an explicit tint/shader or a lit plane. Test sprite/roof/wall occlusion, alpha edges, foot anchor and overlap from both sides of a doorway; billboard depth sorting is a risk, not a solved fact.

## 3. Light as a mechanic

**First rule.** Give each tile a server-calculated light band. Outdoor day is lit; outdoor night, roofed interiors and the undercroft have zero ambient light. A street lamp has a four-tile radius at night; a brazier/altar burns continuously with a three-tile radius; a switched-on carried test lantern has a three-tile radius. Warm colors identify fire, not a different power level. Radius uses the existing Chebyshev tile distance (`packages/game/src/model.ts:8`). Within a radius, a source must have an unobstructed same-floor line to the tile; multiple sources combine by the strongest band, with a deterministic color tie-break. Any player's lantern can illuminate another player's surroundings.

A viewer can see a dynamic entity when there is an unobstructed line to it **and** its tile has nonzero light, or it is within one tile of the viewer. The adjacent allowance is tactile awareness: it keeps close combat and escape possible in total darkness. It does not let actors be seen through walls. Static terrain outside the light is mostly dark; the map remains known, not secret. An unlit creature farther away may approach unseen, but does not gain extra damage or a new behavior. A target that becomes unseen is dropped, along with automatic chase; a targeted spell needs a visible target. Walking, retreat and untargeted area spells still work. Area damage can hit an unseen creature, but its hidden position and damage number are not drawn. This is already compatible in spirit with the area's line-of-sight damage (`packages/game/src/engine.ts:353`).

**Server and presentation.** Put source definitions, clock phase, line checks, the light/visibility calculation, lantern toggle command and target enforcement in `packages/game` and use the same `Engine` on the server. `GameView` carries a bounded light/visibility field and filters dynamic players, monsters, corpses and ground items; the client uses that same field for static NPC drawing, labels, minimap markers and click/hover feedback. Gate server target/describe and targeted spell paths, stop existing targets when visibility is lost, and filter or redact effect positions/from-fields for concealed entities; keep self-directed damage feedback. Review adjacent interactions and area-spell feedback for unintended reveals. Current `view()` returns nearby entities and effects without this filtering (`engine.ts:636`), targeting accepts an existing same-floor monster without a visibility check (`engine.ts:277`), and the minimap draws live markers (`apps/client/src/main.ts:661`). This is a play rule, not secrecy against a modified client: the static map is already distributed.

The present `Geography.sight` ignores door state (`packages/game/src/world.ts:61`); the opaque set omits doors (`packages/game/src/model.ts:13`). The new light/visibility line must treat a closed door as opaque and an open one as transparent, and define diagonal corner blocking. Do not silently change unrelated monster AI or existing combat sight. Lit walls are visible at the endpoint, while light does not continue through them. Verify wall, door, corners, floors, a second player's light and the same-tick case where a light goes out during a chase.

For the prototype, the lantern is free, toggleable and has no fuel; that keeps the first change small. **Next experiment, not part of the initial rule:** a lantern consumes five minutes of fuel while lit, shows time remaining and warns at 30 seconds. A cave beetle could acquire a lit player one tile farther away, while retaining its normal aggro in darkness; thus light buys sight but risks attention. Current acquisition uses distance and sight (`engine.ts:581`), so test this separately before changing it. No accuracy penalties, darkness damage, sanity meter or permanent blind state.

## 4. Day and night

Use a 48-minute active-world cycle: dawn 0–6 minutes, day 6–24, dusk 24–30, night 30–48. Derive a shared phase from authoritative `state.time`, not each browser's wall clock. The current server pauses ticks when nobody is present or combat-locked (`apps/server/src/index.ts:62`); therefore this proposal intentionally pauses the cycle too. Persistence already stores game time (`packages/game/src/model.ts:47`); a real-world continuous clock would be a separate decision.

Fade color and sun direction through dawn and dusk; quantize the authoritative ambient into four bands. On a band transition, visibility changes with the server field immediately; decorative color may interpolate, but must not show an actor the server hides. Day is parchment and moss (`#e8cf99`, `#536b42`), dusk copper and violet (`#d77b3b`, `#49435f`), night blue-black and lantern gold (`#101d30`, `#f3a04c`), with turquoise reserved for a rare magical accent. Street lamps switch on at the night band and off on returning to daylight; windows get decorative warm pixels on the same schedule. Braziers and the altar stay on. This requires updating the current promise that street-lamp flames never go out (`engine.ts:546`). Undercroft light does not follow the sky.

The steep camera conveys the time through ground and roof color, direction and length of shadows, lit windows, water glints and lamp pools. Moon and sparse stars belong in the optional lookout vista; no sky needs to be visible during ordinary travel. There are no weather or season systems in this proposal.

## 5. Grandeur

The reference cathedral works through a dark foreground, a warm detailed city and a pale distant silhouette; the dragon works through a tiny rider against one immense shape. The moon scenes pair deep blue with a few orange pools. The current screenshot instead puts similarly sized block figures amid dense, high-frequency grass. Borrow the compositions and color relationships, not names, images or landmarks.

At the existing north river and bridge (`packages/game/src/town.ts:41`, `:62`), frame a quiet road with two tall trees, keep the player and bridge readable in the middle, and paint a huge distant lantern tower and hazy hills beyond the playable boundary. Suggest trees or tower sections five to eight player heights tall. Spend art on that one dominant silhouette rather than adding many large props. A player-triggered lookout opens a shallow, pixel-painted sky/vista panel across the top of the scene, with a moon at night and slow clouds; it is explicitly non-interactive and outside the tile map. The live world continues below it; dismiss the vista on movement or combat, and do not pretend it pauses multiplayer. Start with a fixed painted layer rather than assuming that tilting an orthographic camera will reveal a real horizon. Atmospheric depth is painted into the backdrop, not a global fog that can conceal threats. No new continent, giant monster, free camera or cutscene yet.

## 6. Feel and weight

The server commits a new tile immediately and only then sets the next allowed move time (`packages/game/src/engine.ts:460`); the existing renderer interpolates from the old tile after receiving that update (`apps/client/src/render/view.ts:331`, `:447`). For this brief's stricter truthfulness, snap the actor's **foot anchor and pick target to the newly confirmed tile** on receipt. Animate two footfall poses and a small upper-body lean within that tile, bounded to 0.1 tile, for the remaining confirmed step interval. Diagonals keep their server timing. On delayed/coalesced updates or floor changes, snap rather than inventing intermediate occupied tiles. This may feel more stepped than current interpolation; prototype it rather than claiming it will feel good.

Use received `Effect` events for all impacts (`packages/game/src/model.ts:70`): an immediate impact pixel and 40–60 ms local pose hold, 0.1-tile recoil/squash and at most one internal-pixel camera nudge. Never freeze simulation or displace the actual actor. The existing ember starts a 260 ms travel animation *after* the effect arrives (`apps/client/src/render/view.ts:384`, `:523`); replace it with an impact on receipt and a short trailing spark, so damage is not visually delayed. An input can show a noncommittal wind-up, but no strike, damage or spell hit before confirmation. Use a short footfall dust pixel and a server-confirmed corpse transition; the hero currently respawns immediately on death (`packages/game/src/engine.ts:427`), so don't show a death animation that delays or obscures respawn. Offer reduced motion and no flash. Sound is desirable later, not essential to this visual decision.

## 8. Risks and measurement

**Performance.** These are targets, not measured results: on a named MacBook-class integrated GPU, run 60-second repeatable town, cave and bridge scenarios at 1080p, 1440p and 4K window sizes, recording browser/version, DPR, zoom, internal size, actor count, and warmup. Aim for 60 fps, 95th-percentile frame time ≤16.7 ms and 1% low ≥45 fps. Capture frame times and draw calls across *all* render passes; GPU timings only where supported. Compare current and candidate at the same settings. First budget is a 300–360-pixel internal height and 1024 directional shadow map; remove mist and reduce the shadow before reducing readability. Point-shadow performance, art throughput and all these targets remain unverified.

The server also needs measurement: at up to the room's 16 clients (`apps/server/src/index.ts:38`), aim for total tick p95 <25 ms and p99 <50 ms under the same scenario, including view generation. A 45×45 one-byte-per-tile field is roughly 2 KB per view, or 324 KB/s at 16 clients and 10 views/s **before** serialization and other data. It covers the current near-entity radius of less than 22 tiles (`packages/game/src/engine.ts:638`); viewport behavior beyond it and actual payload size must be measured. Compute shared floor lighting once per relevant change, then viewer visibility; any cache must invalidate on movement, door, source and phase changes and remain correct across server transaction rollback (`apps/server/src/index.ts:33`). Do not optimize by ignoring a gameplay light source.

**Readability and accessibility.** Always show the player, the adjacent traversable area and the boundary of nearby walls; darkness removes distant information, not controls. Give a brightness calibration and high-contrast terrain/visible-actor option without expanding the server visibility mask. Distinguish targets, light and danger by shape as well as color. Test the cave in a bright room, on a dim display and in color-vision simulations; reduced flash, shake and motion must be available. Do not reveal hidden enemy names through the battle list, minimap or accessibility setting.

**Art and compatibility.** One hero and boar are a cost probe, not evidence that a full bestiary is affordable. If their silhouettes do not read at play size, fix composition before adding frames. A light toggle or field changes protocol/state; existing saves are parsed at startup, and a failed parse can reset the world (`apps/server/src/index.ts:22`). An eventual rollout needs backward-compatible defaults or an explicit migration and representative saved-world checks. No save migration or production activation is authorized by this planning proposal.

3. **River overlook — 3–5 days.** Add `proto/overlook/` page and original placeholder vista art, reusing the candidate renderer and shared local Engine. No weather, expanded map, cinematic system, fuel economy or production renderer migration.

## What changed in pass 2

1. **Engine:** Kept Three.js, but removed the speculative combination of eight leaking point lights plus a mask and the default point-shadow trial. One authoritative tile field is simpler and cannot disagree with gameplay light; six-face shadow cost was checked in the installed renderer.
2. **Actors:** Kept four-direction painted sprites, but cut the initial art commitment from two complete sheets to 20 test frames. Checked the installed sprite shader and made explicit that sprites need their own light sampling; depth sorting remains a prototype risk.
3. **Light:** Specified exact radii, adjacency, target loss, area-spell behavior, field ownership and view/action filtering. Reopened sight and found that closed doors do not currently block it; added a separate door-aware light/visibility line and a multiplayer check. Fuel and AI remain deferred.
4. **Day/night:** Kept the 48-minute active-time cycle and clarified band transitions, roofed interiors, always-burning braziers and the server's idle pause. Street-lamp text must change when their schedule does.
5. **Grandeur:** Replaced the unproven camera-tilt horizon with a dismissible painted lookout, because an orthographic ground view does not by itself guarantee a useful sky. Kept the bridge, huge silhouette and layered composition.
6. **Feel:** Replaced the previous between-tiles interpolation with a confirmed-tile foot anchor after checking the server commits movement before the current animation. Clarified immediate respawn and effect-timed impacts.
7. **Prototypes:** Reduced four to three and combined visual light with the opt-in shared rule, so there is one light calculation. Increased estimates to cover the actual visibility, protocol and behavior work; they remain unverified.
8. **Risks:** Added a 16-client server and bandwidth budget, cache/rollback concern, actual live pixel-height finding, and save compatibility risk. The frame-rate target remains a goal, not a measured result.
