# Lanternfall visuals — Terra, pass 1

Keep Three.js and make a painted-pixel world inside the existing tile game: hand-drawn actors and large architectural silhouettes, a quiet low-resolution palette, and pools of warm light against blue darkness. Prove the art and light in four small local-Engine prototypes. Make one server-owned visibility rule before considering fuel or smarter monsters. The aim is the emotional scale of the cathedral and dragon references, and the uncertainty of the flashlight reference, without changing the authoritative tile game.

## 1. Engine

Keep Three.js 0.187.0 (`package.json:27`). Nothing in the request needs an engine change: its existing geometry and shadow maps are valuable for roofs, walls and stairs, while sprites can be drawn in the same scene. A 2D engine would require rebuilding floor switching, hit picking, roof cutaways, labels, style controls and the post pass; that cost buys no capability the current stack lacks. No engine shootout until the prototypes reveal a concrete failure.

`WorldView` already has an orthographic camera, low-resolution color/depth target, sun and point-light pool (`apps/client/src/render/view.ts:61`, `:103`); its resize and nearest target plus pixelated canvas give a starting pixel grid (`view.ts:236`, `apps/client/src/style.css:31`). Its depth outlines, noise and blanket warm/cool grading (`view.ts:22`) should be reduced, not relied upon to invent pixel art. Keep the directional shadow for day; adjust sun and ambient for time and floor. Replace freely leaking lamp illumination with a tile light field built from authoritative source data and line of sight, applied to terrain and actor shaders. Quantize intensity to four bands; a tiny flicker affects presentation only. Start with the current nearest eight unshadowed light pool for highlights and use the tile field to block their spill. Test at most one optional shadow-casting carried point light, never a shadow map for every lamp. Three's own manual says point shadows render six directions, a significant cost ([Three.js shadows](https://threejs.org/manual/pages/shadows.html)). A cheap contact ellipse grounds each sprite. Use two or three slow planar fog layers and particles, not actual volumetric ray marching; a painted lookout backdrop supplies sky when appropriate.

Prototype a renderer copy under `apps/client/proto/`, not a modification to production `WorldView`. Preserve `update(GameView)`, `onPick`, `applyStyle`, labels, roof and floor behavior in the candidate. The current interface also exposes `beforePick`, `tileAtPoint`, `onHover`, `onEffect` and its canvas; production migration must account for these (`view.ts:78`, `:99`, `:248`; `apps/client/src/main.ts:267`). The engine decision is made at prototype 1: retain Three.js if the character, roofs and lit terrain work together at budget; investigate a switch only if that fails after profiling.

## 2. Actors and art

Use hand-pixelled four-direction sprite sheets on camera-facing planes, foot-anchored in world space. Four facings match the fixed camera and constrain art cost; diagonal moves pick the nearest facing, while grid direction and server position remain exact. Prototype a 32×48 hero and 48×32 thornboar, with per direction one idle, four walk poses, two attack poses, one hit and one death pose: 36 frames per actor, with mirror reuse where asymmetry allows. Establish a 16–24 color palette per character, readable cloak/weapon and the boar's tusk silhouette; block in one large tree and one lamp in matching inked pixel shapes. A developer can draw the first two small sheets manually; they are intentionally throwaway, not a claim of finished art. Judge them at actual in-game size, not zoomed editor size.

Do not create normal maps initially. Actor sprite albedo is sampled nearest-neighbor and multiplied by the colored tile illumination at its feet, plus controlled sky contribution. A visible nearby threat has a minimum contrast outline; a hidden threat has neither sprite nor label. Use alpha cutout and depth-tested planes, plus a short contact shadow; check that walls can occlude sprites and that the foot anchors sort correctly. Pre-rendered models and direction-dependent normal maps may be reconsidered if the first two sheets take too long or look flat.

## 3. Light as a mechanic

Baseline: surface world light moves through a day; undercroft ambient stays almost zero. Lamps and braziers have position, radius and warm color; a carried lantern gives a radius of three tiles. Sources illuminate other players as well. The smallest meaningful rule is **visibility, not damage**: an actor within one tile is always discernible; farther away, the player can see and select it only if its tile is lit and there is line of sight. An unlit creature can approach before emerging; no accuracy penalty, damage-over-time or new monster AI in this first rule. Area spells still work by their area and existing line of sight, including unseen targets. Walk and retreat remain possible. A lightless character can still see their tile and adjacent terrain.

Implement the clock, source definitions, illumination/visibility query and action checks in `packages/game`; the same `Engine` instance drives the local prototype and later the server. Emit a compact server-computed field and visible entities in `GameView`, with consistent filtering of labels, battle list and target actions; drop an existing target when it becomes invisible. Do not let a client send a concealed target ID and attack it anyway. Current views expose all nearby monsters (`packages/game/src/engine.ts:636`) and current targeting checks floor and protection zone only (`engine.ts:277`), so both must change. View filtering is a gameplay contract, not an anti-cheat guarantee: the map is already available client-side and visual darkness is not cryptographic secrecy. Color, flicker, shape and edges are presentation only.

After this rule is tested, add a lantern with five minutes of active fuel, a visible gauge and warning at 30 seconds; relighting uses a stocked fuel item. Carrying a light makes the player conspicuous; later, one cave creature can gain +1 acquisition tile for lit players. Do not include fuel or AI in the first mechanics prototype. The current creature aggro is range plus sight (`engine.ts:581`); changing it must be a separate measured experiment.

## 4. Day and night

Use a 48-minute active-world cycle: dawn 6, day 18, dusk 6, night 18 minutes. Derive phase from server game time, shared by everyone; this existing clock pauses when no one is connected or combat-locked (`apps/server/src/index.ts:62`), which is acceptable for the first version. Smoothly interpolate light colors over each transition and quantize the final image. Day: cream sky, muted moss and slate shadows. Dusk: amber highlights, violet shade. Night: desaturated blue-black ground and gold lamp pools; an occasional turquoise wisp is a rare accent. Undercroft ignores surface phase.

At dusk street lamps and window pixels fade on; at dawn they fade out, while braziers remain burning. This requires replacing the current description that every street lamp's flame never goes out (`packages/game/src/engine.ts:546`). No actual window interiors initially. On a steep camera the phase reads from ground shadows, roof colors, water glints, lamps and long silhouettes; moon and stars appear only in the lookout backdrop, not pasted over every ground view. No background clock, weather or seasons.

## 5. Grandeur

The current grass and equal-sized boxes in `current-game.png` bury both the figure and horizon. The reference cathedral earns its scale with a dark foreground, dense warm midground and a distant pale skyline; the dragon earns it by putting an enormous silhouette beside a tiny rider. The moon scenes use one dominant disk and slow firelight. Adopt these relationships, not their assets.

Build one playable river/bridge overlook on the existing map and add a non-colliding painted silhouette of a huge lantern tower beyond the playable boundary. Foreground trees frame a quieter road, the midground contains the player and river, and two parallax background layers recede into blue haze. At the lookout only, allow a player-triggered, non-combat camera tilt/zoom that reveals a narrow strip of pixel-painted sky and the far tower. Keep ordinary travel at the fixed steep angle and preserve input mapping. A roofline or tree trunk 5–8 player heights high gives a scale cue even without a horizon. Distant backdrop and cloud shadows are decorative and never hide entities or imply accessible tiles. No dragon, new huge map, or free camera for the first trial.

## 6. Feel and weight

`Engine` commits a step to the new tile immediately and sets its next permitted move time (`packages/game/src/engine.ts:460`); the current view interpolates afterward (`apps/client/src/render/view.ts:331`, `:447`). Keep the server tile as the only pick, target and collision location. Animate the sprite within the step interval with a planted contact pose and two walking beats, but never extend beyond the two adjacent tiles; snap on floor changes. Add a two-pixel dust puff at the confirmed foot plant and a small landing squash inside the actor's own tile. If updates arrive late, snap or shorten, never invent a path.

On an authoritative `hit`, `miss`, `block`, or `ember` effect (`packages/game/src/model.ts:70`), play the appropriate pose immediately: 40–60 ms local pose hold, up to 0.1 tile squash/recoil and 1–2 internal-pixel camera nudge, then recover. No global simulation pause, displacement of server positions, or predicted damage. A cast may have a quiet pre-ack wind-up but no impact until confirmed. The current ember projectile starts after the server event and arrives visually later (`view.ts:384`, `:523`); use an impact at receipt, with a short trailing streak rather than a late false impact. Death transitions to the server corpse when it appears; do not fake an early kill. Make impact and camera motion optional/reduced. Sound is useful but not essential to this decision; defer it.

## 8. Risks and budgets

Set a target, not a claim: 60 fps at 1080p and 1440p on a named MacBook-class integrated GPU, with 95th-percentile frame time ≤16.7 ms after warmup; investigate any 1% low below 45 fps. Use the same scenario, zoom and device-pixel ratio at each resolution, capture 60 seconds of town, bridge and cave, record frame-time distribution, draw calls and GPU timing if supported. No performance measurement was run in this pass. The canvas currently runs at a fraction of host size (`view.ts:236`); actual scene, shadow-map and CSS costs still need measurement. Cap internal height at 360 initially, shadow map at 1024 and active decorative lights at eight; keep one optional carried shadow only if measured. If it misses budget, remove that shadow first, then fog/dust; never lower the visibility guarantee.

Darkness should hide distant threats, not make the player lose their feet: adjacent navigable tiles, own sprite, exits and UI retain legible contrast. Provide a brightness calibration and an accessibility contrast mode that lifts terrain, plus reduced flashes/shake, without increasing server-granted sight. Check common color-vision deficiencies with icon/shape distinctions as well as hue. Art risk is the 36-frame-per-actor commitment: validate one hero and one boar first and cost them in actual hours; asset quality and production throughput remain unverified. Do not expand the bestiary before those are usable.

## 7. Prototypes, in order

All pages live under `apps/client/proto/<name>/`, use the real local `Engine` like `apps/client/proto/chrome/main.ts:17`, and are not production imports. Sizes are rough developer-days, not measured estimates.

1. **Painted actor / engine gate — 2–3 days.** Add `proto/painted/{index.html,main.ts,renderer.ts,art/*}`. One hero, one boar, a tree and lamp in the real town; current vs candidate toggle. Proves a sprite/geometry hybrid and decides whether Three.js stays. Check recognizable poses at native size, foot anchor, tile picking, movement, roof hiding and both floors; measure budget. No art library or production rewrite.
2. **Darkness and occlusion — 2–4 days.** Add `proto/light/{index.html,main.ts,renderer.ts}` and prototype assets. Show lamp, carried light and a wall around a cave corner, a toggle for the expensive shadow option, and a debug display of tile light. Judge that lit/unlit boundaries agree at wall edges, colors stay crisp, and the 60-second timing meets budget. Visual presentation only until rule 3; do not advertise its slider as game authority.
3. **Shared visibility rule — 3–5 days.** Add `proto/sight/` and an opt-in shared rule implementation in `packages/game/src/{model,world,engine}.ts`; keep production defaults unchanged until the prototype wins. Two local players share one Engine, one with a lantern: compare each authoritative view, target attempts, loss of target and an approaching creature. Check no hidden creature appears in its battle list or can be targeted by ID; adjacent escape and area spell still work. Add focused behavior checks for sight around a wall and multiplayer sharing. Do not add fuel, AI changes or a new combat system.
4. **Bridge at dusk — 2–4 days.** Add `proto/overlook/{index.html,main.ts,renderer.ts,art/*}`; show a phase scrubber, window/lamp transitions and one optional lookout camera with painted sky, tower and water. Check that a player can locate their tile and walk from bridge to town at every phase, roof cutaways work, and a static screenshot shows a clear foreground, middle ground and distant landmark. No new world map, weather, dynamic clouds or cinematics.
