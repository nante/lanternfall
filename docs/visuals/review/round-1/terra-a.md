# Lanternfall visual prototypes — final review

**Score: 5.5/10**

The fork is a promising visual direction, but it is not ready to migrate. Its warm pools, teal shrine, nocturnal eyes and low camera give Lanternfall a more distinctive fable-like identity than Today; the moonlit bridge and golden gate are especially evocative. The scenery is still comparatively simple and geometric beside the references' layered, monumental compositions, while the unlit undercroft is almost featureless. More seriously, floor changes can desynchronize the light field, dark items remain visible, and the lab cannot yet establish whether darkness is playable. Keep the Three.js fork and the continuum/lookout comparison, fix those problems, and let the user make the visual choice after the acceptance checks are exercised.

## Ranked findings

1. **Changing floors can leave the surface light field using underground sources.** `apps/client/proto/_next/lightfield.ts:138` binds a floor's mask and ambient textures, but the source positions and colors are shared global uniforms. `:146` only rewrites those uniforms when that floor's cached source key changes. After surface → undercroft → surface at a fixed night time, the surface mask is rebound while the uniforms still contain the underground sources. In a live isolated renderer on the running lab, `readLevels()` agreed with `lightGrid()` before the switch and underground, then disagreed on **825 surface tiles**, including **41 within eight tiles horizontally and five vertically** of the market player. A repeated `setWorld()` at the same state still had 825 mismatches; advancing six seconds until a source moved rebuilt the uniforms and restored agreement. The picture can therefore show different light from the server's rule during ordinary floor transitions. Restore each field's source uniforms whenever it is bound, or give each floor its own uniforms; verify an unchanged-source round trip, not just independent initial scenes.

2. **Ground items remain visible and identifiable on tiles the player cannot make out.** `packages/game/src/engine.ts:679` sends nearby ground items without the visibility filter used for corpses at `:698`; `apps/client/proto/_next/view.ts:451` draws them using an unlit `SpriteMaterial`. With darkness enabled and no torch, a gold pile at (54,45,1) was sent and visibly rendered using the game's real icon while the player stood at (51,45,1); `describe()` returned “It is too dark to make out.” This is within the plan's central light-as-information goal, although ground-item redaction is not separately named in its rule list. Filter ground items by the same visible-tile decision as corpses when darkness is enabled, and light their sprites with the field so a permitted dim item does not glow independently.

3. **The lab cannot perform the required playability check.** `apps/client/proto/lab/main.ts:96` supplies engine views to the renderer, while `apps/client/proto/lab/harness.ts:29` only drives an optional automatic hunt. The renderer's `onPick` remains a no-op at `apps/client/proto/_next/view.ts:175`, and the lab has no movement/action key handler. In the live darkness scene, a canvas click and ArrowUp did not move the scene; `-` and `=` each left the stop at Play, despite the plan requiring those keys. A user cannot walk, fight, or test recovery from darkness, so the “scary but playable” acceptance check remains open. Wire the existing local engine actions to picking and keyboard input, including the planned stop keys, and run the torch-on/off walk and combat in the lab.

4. **Labels appear through scenery walls.** `apps/client/proto/_next/actor-layer.ts:291` shows DOM labels based on projected screen bounds; `:262` handles roof hiding but does not test intervening walls. In two real-time gate Scenery captures, “Ash” and “Warden Corin” appear on the solid left wall while their figures are occluded. This undermines the depth and place that Scenery is intended to prove. Hide a label when intervening world geometry occludes its actor, using the existing camera/depth information.

5. **Reduced motion retains planned optional motion.** `apps/client/proto/_next/feel.ts:19` removes hit-stop, knockback, kick and flash but retains dust and collapse; sparks are also emitted unconditionally at `apps/client/proto/_next/actor-layer.ts:222`. The plan says reduced motion turns the listed effects off. In a live Chrome session emulating `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`, the Figures log confirmed reduced motion and the active tuning returned `dust: true` and `collapse: true`. Disable the remaining optional particles and collapse under reduced motion while retaining a clear, server-confirmed indication of the outcome.

## What I verified

I read `docs/visuals/BRIEF.md`, `BUILD-PLAN.md`, `.audit/visuals-build.todo.md`, `README.md`, the specified fork and lab code, the game rule changes and their tests. I opened all 13 supplied 1200×700 shots and the eight reference/contact images. I captured my own real-time market, figures, unlit undercroft and gate shots with `node apps/client/proto/lab/eval.mjs ... 'shot:/tmp/lanternfall-terra-a/…'`; the gate was captured again in pass 2. Scratch browser probes in that same temp directory instantiated the actual `Engine`, `WorldView` and `LightField` against the running lab, exercised the floor round trip, and placed a real gold icon in the dark. These were controlled local instances, not the networked world.

- `labVerify()` at noon market: **0 mismatches, 5 sources**. This establishes the initial scene only; the separate round-trip probe above found the cache defect.
- `labDarkCheck(15000)` after initialization, unlit undercroft: **60 samples, 0 full, 360 shades, 0 disagreements**. Its implementation at `apps/client/proto/lab/main.ts:163` compares shared-rule calculations to the engine view; it does not read the GPU picture or drive a walk, so it cannot establish the plan's picture-versus-server walk check. An earlier heavily loaded startup run reported five disagreements; I could not reproduce them after initialization and did not treat them as a game-rule defect.
- Figures auto-hunt with `feel=off`: after 21 seconds, **74 effects, 74 strikes, 0 logged early strikes, 1,254 ring checks, 0 off-tile**. This is not the required five-minute run, and the logger's own check does not independently timestamp server receipt.
- At 1920×1080, the lab's synchronous `labBench()` reported **0.70 ms median / 1.50 ms p95** at the night gate and **0.90 / 1.20 ms** in golden gate Scenery. These measurements include its `frame()`/`gl.finish()` loop, not a representative full networked workload or independent GPU profiling.
- `npm test`: **82 passed**; `npm run typecheck`: passed. The 16-hero test printed **+0.538 ms** per batch on this run, but logs rather than asserts the threshold. Its flag-off seeded comparison uses false versus an absent flag in the current engine, not an archived pre-change engine.

I did not verify all five requested field phases and torch combinations, all pixel-lock sizes and DPRs, the five-minute hunt, manual or two-hero torch play, every scenery location and transition, recognition by the user, brightness on a dim display, other GPUs, or an actual production migration. The user has not judged the continuum against the lookout or the darkness as playable; those acceptance checks remain open.
