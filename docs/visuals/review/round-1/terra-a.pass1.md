# Lanternfall visual prototypes — pass 1

**Score: 6/10**

The fork is a credible visual direction, not yet a migration candidate. The palette, pools of warm light, nocturnal silhouettes and low camera give Lanternfall more identity than Today; the bridge moon and gate skyline suggest a fable rather than a filtered voxel scene. The scenery still feels like a small geometric stage beside the layered scale and painted detail of the references, and the unlit undercroft is almost featureless. More importantly, the lab does not let a reviewer play the darkness scenario, and items can visibly contradict the darkness rule. Keep the Three.js fork and compare the continuum with the lookout, but fix the truth and reviewability gaps before asking the user to choose or migrating it.

## Ranked findings

1. **Dark ground items disclose themselves in an otherwise unseen tile.** `packages/game/src/engine.ts:679` sends all nearby ground items without the visibility filter applied to corpses at `:698`, while `apps/client/proto/_next/view.ts:451` draws their sprites with an unlit `SpriteMaterial`. In a live, isolated lab-renderer setup, a gold item at (54,45,1) appeared as a bright square from (51,45,1) with no torch; `describe()` said “It is too dark to make out,” yet the view sent `gold` and rendered the sprite. The synthetic yellow icon makes the problem obvious; the material is unlit regardless of icon. This weakens the central light mechanic and leaks item details to a client when the tile cannot be made out. Smallest fix: filter ground items by the same tile visibility rule as corpses when darkness is enabled, and ensure their material does not glow independently of the field.

2. **The lab cannot perform the required playability check.** `apps/client/proto/lab/main.ts:96` only sends local engine views to the renderer; `apps/client/proto/lab/harness.ts:29` drives the optional automatic hunt, and the renderer's `onPick` remains its no-op default at `apps/client/proto/_next/view.ts:175`. There is no lab movement/action keyboard handler. Clicking or walking therefore cannot explore, fight or recover from darkness, although the canvas advertises these actions at `view.ts:187`. The required “scary but playable” judgment and scripted player walk are not established by stills or auto-hunt. Wire the existing pick and movement actions to the local engine, then exercise the torch-on/off walk and combat in the actual lab.

3. **Feel Off does not turn the attack feedback off.** `apps/client/proto/_next/actor-layer.ts:258` advances the swing regardless of feel level, and `:214` starts the strike regardless of tuning; `:222` emits sparks independently as well. With `tab=figures&at=meadow&t=19.4&feel=off&hunt=1`, the live log after 21 seconds reported 74 effects, 74 strikes and 71 wind-ups. That prevents the promised Off/Subtle/Heavy comparison from isolating the contribution of the new weight. Gate optional animation and particles under Off while preserving authoritative timing for the enabled modes; apply the plan's reduced-motion behavior consistently too.

4. **Labels show through scenery walls.** `apps/client/proto/_next/actor-layer.ts:291` positions DOM labels when the actor is in projected screen bounds, without checking intervening depth; `:262` only checks roofs. In the real-time gate Scenery shot, “Ash” and “Warden Corin” are written over the solid left wall although their figures are obscured. This breaks the depth and sense of place the Scenery prototype is meant to prove. Hide labels whose actor is occluded by the intervening world geometry, using the existing camera/depth information.

## What I verified

Read the user brief, build plan, coordinator trail, README, the renderer and lab code, the light and darkness rules and tests. Opened all 13 supplied shots and the eight reference/contact images. Took real-time 1200×700 lab shots in `/tmp/lanternfall-terra-a/` of market, figures, the unlit undercroft and the gate Scenery; the gate shot was repeated without parallel startup load. A scratch browser setup constructed an isolated engine and renderer to place a ground item in darkness, and returned the view, description and rendered-item count. `labVerify()` at noon market returned `0 mismatches · 5 sources`. A fully initialized `labDarkCheck(15000)` in the no-torch undercroft returned 60 samples, 0 full, 360 shades and 0 disagreements. An earlier run started under heavy parallel browser load and reported 5 disagreements; I do not yet treat those as a rule defect. `npm test` passed 82 tests, and `npm run typecheck` passed. The 16-hero test printed +0.538 ms per batch in this run, but it does not assert the budget.

Not verified: a five-minute hunt; subjective recognition by the user; every stop and every location; a scripted player walk (the lab has no input wiring); all five field-check phases; the required 1080p frame budgets and all pixel-lock resolutions; interactive two-hero torch play; performance on other GPUs; an actual migration into production. The seeded flag-off test compares false to an absent flag in the current engine, not a saved pre-change engine, so it does not independently prove a deep-equal comparison to today's implementation.
