# Lanternfall visuals review — final

**Score: 6/10**

The prototypes are a substantial visual improvement over Today. The baked figures read more like pixel art, the amber lamps and teal shrine give light a distinctive role, and the night meadow and oversized moon suggest a compelling fable. I would keep the Three.js direction and the camera experiment for further evaluation. The Scenery views do not yet achieve the layered scale and striking focal subject of the supplied references: the small keep and repeated triangular ridges read as a backdrop rather than a vast place. More importantly, the supplied lab cannot be played, and the renderer can disagree with server light or fail to select a nearby shade. These are blockers to the planned comparison and to treating the fork as ready to migrate, rather than reasons to discard the visual direction.

## Ranked findings

1. **Returning to a floor can use the other floor's light sources — `apps/client/proto/_next/lightfield.ts:137`, `apps/client/proto/_next/lightfield.ts:146`.** Each floor caches its own mask, but `uFSrc` and `uFCol` are global and are rewritten only when that floor's source key changes. Binding an unchanged floor restores its textures but leaves the other floor's source uniforms in place. In the live page I drove an actual `WorldView` with a local `Engine` through surface → undercroft → surface at a fixed night time. The GPU field then differed from `lightGrid` on 825 of the 84×68 surface tiles; the 23×19 rectangle around the south-gate focus contained 124 mismatches. A separate controlled readback changed a level-6 source to level 1 on the return while the rule stayed at 6. Light and the server's full/shade decision can consequently disagree after travel. Store and bind source uniforms with each field, or force a source upload whenever the bound floor changes; include a round-trip check.

2. **The lab cannot be played — `apps/client/proto/lab/harness.ts:37`, `apps/client/proto/_next/view.ts:175`.** The harness advances and optionally auto-targets the local engine, but never wires the view's default no-op `onPick` to an engine action; it also has no keyboard movement handler. In both the undercroft and meadow Darkness scenes, a ground click followed by an arrow key left the stationary friends' screen positions unchanged. This prevents a person from walking, targeting, opening a door, or evaluating whether darkness is scary *and playable*, and prevents the planned picture-versus-server walk from being run through the interface. Wire the existing view callbacks and movement keys to the currently observed local hero, including action errors, while retaining Scenery's click-to-return behavior.

3. **An adjacent shade is legal to target but cannot be picked as an actor — `apps/client/proto/_next/view.ts:357`, `apps/client/proto/_next/shades.ts:39`.** Shade IDs are drawn by `ShadeLayer`, while picking searches only `ActorLayer`'s full actors. In a live local-engine scenario an adjacent dark rat appeared in `view.shades`, and the engine accepted `target` for its ID; clicking its projected position produced the correct ground tile but no `actor` field. Even after lab controls are connected, the visible threat cannot be selected through the renderer's click contract. Include sent shades in picking, returning their server ID and tile without revealing kind or name; let the server continue to reject out-of-range shades.

4. **The picture-agreement probe does not check the picture — `apps/client/proto/lab/main.ts:163`.** `labDarkCheck` recomputes rule levels and compares them with the server's full and shade lists. It reads neither the GPU field nor the rendered actor/shade state, and the default scenes do not walk. The no-torch cave run reported 60 samples, 360 shades and zero disagreements, but that result cannot detect finding 1 or 3. The claimed zero picture/server disagreements is therefore unproven. Exercise an actual walk and compare GPU field readback and drawn actor identities/positions with each server view, including a door and floor round trip.

5. **Shade movement does not snap after a skipped update — `apps/client/proto/_next/shades.ts:56`, `apps/client/proto/_next/shades.ts:69`.** The shade renderer eases 25% of the remaining distance every frame regardless of how many tiles the server advanced. In a live module recheck, changing a dim shade from (40,59) to (43,59) drew it at (40.75,59) on the next frame. With delayed or merged snapshots, the eyes can remain multiple tiles away from the actual threat; full figures explicitly snap on nonadjacent changes. Apply the same snap rule to shades, and use a bounded, time-based adjacent step if glide is desired.

## What I verified

- Read the visuals brief, build plan, audit trail, shared light/darkness code and tests, and the relevant renderer and lab code. Inspected all 13 supplied shots and the eight reference/contact images. I captured and viewed my own real-time 1200×700 shots with `node apps/client/proto/lab/eval.mjs '<query>' 'shot:/tmp/lanternfall-terra-b/<name>.png' 5000 1200 700`: gate Scenery at golden hour, unlit undercroft Darkness, and Figures at dusk. The browser also opened and interacted with the running lab on port 4173.
- Ran `labVerify()` at market noon, gate golden hour, shrine blue hour, meadow night and undercroft with a torch: respectively 0 mismatches with 5, 5, 22, 25 and 7 sources. These were fresh, single-floor scenes. The separate live `WorldView`/`Engine` round-trip probe above used `readLevels` against `lightGrid` and exposed the cached-uniform defect.
- Ran `labDarkCheck(15000)` in the undercroft with a torch (60 samples, 240 full, 0 shades, 0 disagreements) and without one (60 samples, 0 full, 360 shades, 0 disagreements). Both only establish the limited comparison described in finding 4. Reproduced adjacent-shade picking against the actual engine and renderer; rechecked shade jump interpolation in the live browser.
- `npm test` passed all 82 tests; `npm run typecheck` passed. The 16-hero darkness benchmark is a logged measurement rather than an assertion: it printed +6.285 ms under concurrent browser/test load and +1.841 ms in a separate run with browser probes also active. Neither establishes the ≤2 ms acceptance budget in a controlled run.
- Headless `labBench()` at 1920×1080 measured night market median 0.60 ms, p95 1.20 ms and night bridge Scenery median 0.60 ms, p95 1.30 ms, with 25 selected sources. This measures the script's repeated `frame()` plus `gl.finish()`, not an entire end-to-end game tick, a sustained interactive session, or startup baking. The tracked diff showed no changes under `apps/client/src` or `apps/server`.

Not verified: the five-minute auto-hunt timing and target-ring log; the full pixel-lock resolution/DPR matrix; user recognition and choice of figures or scenery; the user's scary-but-playable judgment; performance across representative hardware or a controlled 16-hero run. A zero from the existing darkness probe does not fill those gaps. I did not start, stop, or restart a server, install packages, or edit implementation files.

## What changed in pass 2

I rechecked every pass-1 finding against code and the live lab. The floor-uniform issue was strengthened from a controlled field-only probe to a full `WorldView`/`Engine` floor round trip; shade movement was rechecked with a three-tile skipped update; the no-input and limited-probe findings were retained. I added the independently reproduced adjacent-shade picking failure and lowered the score from 6.5 to 6. The 32-source limit produced whole-map mismatches in a synthetic crowded scene, but I did not establish a visible-screen failure there, so it is not a ranked finding.
